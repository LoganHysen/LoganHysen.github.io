Landscape ecologist · Conservation scientist

Connecting landscapes, wildlife, and people across scales.

I am a PhD candidate at Michigan State University studying how climate change and human activity shape wildlife habitat—from local landscapes to globally connected systems.

Research

01

I study how ecological and human processes combine to shape habitat and conservation outcomes.

My dissertation examines how climate change and human processes operating at multiple scales affect giant panda habitat. More broadly, I use spatial and quantitative approaches to ask applied, methodological, and theoretical questions across taxa, ecosystems, and geographies.

Research themes

Questions that travel across boundaries

01

Habitat and connectivity

Understanding where species live, how they move, and how changing landscapes connect—or isolate—them.

02

Human–wildlife coexistence

Examining the relationships between human decisions, livelihoods, development, and wildlife conservation.

03

Coupled systems

Tracing ecological and socioeconomic connections across places, scales, and administrative borders.

04

Spatial conservation

Using GIS, reproducible workflows, and quantitative models to support effective conservation decisions.
