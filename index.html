Research

01

I study how ecological and human processes combine to shape habitat and conservation outcomes.

My dissertation examines how climate change and human processes operating at multiple scales affect giant panda habitat. More broadly, I use spatial and quantitative approaches to ask applied, methodological, and theoretical questions across taxa, ecosystems, and geographies.

Research interests

The questions that guide my work

01

Habitat and connectivity

I am interested in how species select habitat and how landscape change affects the connections between populations. My dissertation examines giant panda habitat under climate change and human activity.

02

Human–wildlife coexistence

I study how human activity shapes wildlife habitat, and how conservation can account for the people who share those landscapes.

03

Coupled systems

I use a coupled human-natural systems perspective to understand how processes in one place can affect conservation elsewhere, across local and global scales.

04

Spatial conservation

I use spatial analysis and quantitative models to connect ecological questions with conservation decisions. My northern spotted owl research examines where nesting habitat overlaps with wildfire risk.
